The income proxy

The eASR portal disallows automated access, so ward-level rates were derived from published compilations of the same official schedule: 125 revenue-village localities, manually mapped to wards with a confidence flag, then taken as a median per ward. The spread is 7.3× from M/East (₹53,970/m²) to A ward (₹3,92,624/m²), which is enough to define four meaningful income bands.

It remains land value, not income. Its worst failure — informal settlements inside expensive wards, such as Dharavi in G/North or Behrampada in H/East — is handled by pinning slum-flagged buildings to the lowest band regardless of rate.

The weather record

The daily series splices two sources, visible in the decimal precision: 1951–2019 is a gridded product (April–May median Tmax ≈ 35.9 °C, daily range ≈ 12 °C), 2020–2024 is station data (34.5 °C, range ≈ 8.5 °C). The gridded era is inland-biased and would overstate cooling demand, so only the station era is used. A typical pre-monsoon day is taken as the April–May median and expanded to 24 hours with a Parton–Logan curve.

One consequence: a single series means no urban heat island gradient. Every ward receives identical forcing, and the only spatial variation in indoor temperature comes from building fabric.

## Derived stock

```{python}
import geopandas as gpd, pandas as pd
s = gpd.read_parquet("data/interim/stock_population.parquet")
pd.DataFrame({
    "buildings in Greater Mumbai": [f"{len(s):,}"],
    "floor area": [f"{s.floor_area_m2.sum()/1e6:.1f} km²"],
    "households": [f"{s.households.sum()/1e6:.2f} M"],
    "informal share of buildings": [f"{100*s.is_slum.mean():.1f}%"],
}).T.rename(columns={0: ""})
```
